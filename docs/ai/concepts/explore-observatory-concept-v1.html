The Observatory · A Direction Study

The browsing room next to the salon —
aim it at a place, look through a lens.

Explore is the room beside the Concierge. Where the Salon is a single tall portal you ask a question through, the Observatory is a viewing deck: its centrepiece is a wide meridian band — a horizon slit that tunes toward your destination — with a rail of lenses beneath it (Flights, Stays, Dining, Experiences). You aim the deck at a place and look through a lens to browse a verified editorial index, then keep what you like in your folio. Same materials as the Salon — velvet canvas, brass hairlines, Fraunces italic, the world-tuned scene — but a wider, calmer silhouette. A different room in the same house.

Same world, different room

The Observatory reuses the Salon's exact materials — velvet canvas, brass-foil hairlines, Fraunces italic, the world-DNA scene, the depth/grain/vignette stack, the "kept in your folio" save moment.

The silhouette is deliberately different: a wide, low meridian band with a horizon hairline instead of a tall portal, and a row of lenses instead of a single ask. Browse-led, not ask-led — yet unmistakably the same house.

Honest by construction

Every card is a verified, operating place from the existing Google Places Explore search. Trust reads "Verified place · 1 source" only when the backend confirms it.

Production result objects carry no image field today, so the default card is the typeset plate; a real source photo replaces it only when one exists. No stock photos, no fabricated ratings, awards, distances, or "open now" claims.

Save writes to the folio. Add-to-trip happens downstream in Saved — never minted from Explore.

Mobile-first behaviour

Pre-search fits one screen: short meridian, swipe-tray lenses, thumb-reachable aim field. No long vertical pile, no utilitarian tab chrome.

Looking through a lens collapses the banner and reveals a scannable index; the active lens stays reachable for quick switching. Tap targets ≥44px; ambient drift and the bloom are prefers-reduced-motion gated.

The four lenses map 1:1 to today's verticals — Dining, Stays, Experiences, Flights — so nothing about routing or providers changes.
